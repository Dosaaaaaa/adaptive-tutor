Kinds:
- recall: tests whether the student knows a fact or idea from the notes.
- apply: tests using the idea in a new situation (a calculation with NEW numbers, or a short scenario that is not
  in the notes).
- tiebreak: a fresh question of the same difficulty, on a DIFFERENT fact.
- simple: an easier question on a basic fact.

Return JSON:
{"questions": [{"kind": "recall", "stem": "the question", "correct": "the correct option text",
  "distractors": [{"text": "wrong option", "tag": "M1.1"}, {"text": "wrong option", "tag": "M1.2"},
                  {"text": "wrong option", "tag": "other"}],
  "rationale": "one sentence: why the correct option is right",
  "evidence_quote": "one sentence copied exactly from the notes that supports the correct option"}]}

Rules:
- Exactly 3 distractors per question. Do not write letters or numbers in front of options: the system shuffles them.
- Every question must be answerable from the notes alone. All questions test different facts.
- Tag a distractor with a misconception id from the list when it is what a student with that misconception would
  pick. Otherwise tag it "other". Use at most 2 "other" per question. In recall, apply and tiebreak questions, tag
  at least one distractor with a misconception id.
- No hints. Option text never reveals which one is right. Never write "(Recommended)", "all of the above",
  "none of the above" or "both A and B".
- The correct option must NOT be the longest option. Wrong options must be as plausible, specific and long as the
  correct one. Real mistakes, not silly ones.
- Do not repeat the correct answer inside the question text. Questions must not give away each other's answers.
- evidence_quote is copied exactly, character for character, from the notes.
